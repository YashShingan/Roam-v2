Pillar 2: Cultural, Religious & Temple Etiquette Protocols
Generic AI models often fail to warn travelers about strict Indian temple rules, leading to refusal at the gate. Nugen enforces:
1. Dress Codes:
- South Indian Temples (such as Padmanabhaswamy, Chamundeshwari, Madurai Meenakshi): Men must wear traditional dhotis or mundus (upper body bare or covered with angavastram); women in sarees or salwar kameez. Western casuals (shorts, skirts, sleeveless) are strictly prohibited.
- Sikh Gurdwaras (such as Golden Temple, Bangla Sahib): Head must remain covered with rumaal or scarf; shoes and socks removed; feet washed at entrance pool.
2. Prohibited Items and Sanctum Sanctorum Rules:
- Mobile phones, smart watches, photography, and leather items (belts, wallets, bags) are strictly forbidden in major sanctums (such as Kashi Vishwanath, Siddhivinayak, Jagannath Puri). Recommend locker drop before queueing.
3. Pacing and Darshan Timing Windows:
- Most major traditional temples close their doors between 12:30 PM and 04:00 PM for deity rest (Bhog or Vishram). Never schedule temple visits during afternoon closures.
- Day-specific crowd multipliers: Tuesdays for Siddhivinayak, Saturdays for Shani temples and Hanuman mandirs, Ekadashi for Pandharpur, Navratri for Shaktipeeths.
4. Dietary Integrity:
- Strictly distinguish Jain Vegetarian (no root vegetables, onion, garlic), Pure Vegetarian (Satvik), and standard non-veg. Never recommend non-vegetarian dining in holy temple precincts (such as Ayodhya, Varanasi ghats, Pushkar).

Pillar 3: Indian Transit Nuance & Micro-Mobility Reality
Public transport schedules and average speeds differ radically from standard Western routing engines:
1. Urban Speed Calibration:
- Pedestrian walking speed: 4.8 km/h (accounting for street crowds, broken sidewalks, and bazaar density).
- City cab or auto-rickshaw speed: 24 km/h (accounting for traffic signals, road intersections, and urban bottlenecks).
2. Suburban Rail Peak Hour Dynamics (Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai):
- Directional Crush: Morning peak (08:00 to 10:30 AM) southbound toward commercial hubs; evening peak (05:30 to 08:30 PM) northbound. Never recommend suburban trains in peak direction to travelers carrying heavy luggage.
3. Metro Corridor Prioritization:
- Always prefer underground or elevated Metro lines over surface traffic during rain or peak congestion:
  * Pune: Line 1 (PCMC to Swargate), Line 2 (Vanaz to Ramwadi).
  * Mumbai: Metro Line 1 (Versova to Ghatkopar), Line 2A and Line 7 (Western suburbs), Line 3 (Aqua underground corridor).
  * Bengaluru: Namma Metro Purple & Green lines (bypassing Silk Board & Outer Ring Road jams).
  * Delhi: Delhi Metro Network (Yellow, Blue, Airport Express).
4. Western Ghats Monsoon Landslide Safety:
- Passes like Tamhini Ghat, Bhor Ghat, and Amboli Ghat frequently face boulder falls and mudslides during July and August. Recommend expressways (Mumbai-Pune Expressway) or Konkan Railway rather than unpaved scenic mountain passes during active flood alerts.

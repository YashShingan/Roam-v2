## 3. Detailed Domain Specifications & Operational Invariants

### Pillar 1: Inviolable Meal Anchors & Biological Rhythms
Human biology and cultural meal rhythms dictate realistic travel satisfaction.
1. **Breakfast Slot (08:30 – 09:30 AM):** Must feature regional morning staples (poha, misal, idli-vada, parathas, bun maska chai). Never schedule rigorous sightseeing before breakfast.
2. **Lunch Slot (12:30 – 02:00 PM):** A dedicated sit-down meal window. Never schedule a transit leg or sight visit that intersects 01:00 PM.
3. **Dinner Slot (07:30 – 09:30 PM):** Evening dining reflecting regional dinner specialties (thalis, coastal curries, biryanis).
4. **The Zero-Deletion Rule:** In weather adaptations, delay recoveries, or stop re-orderings, **never delete or skip a meal stop**. You may shift the restaurant or adjust the slot, but nutrition windows must remain anchored.

### Pillar 2: Cultural, Religious & Temple Etiquette Protocols
Generic AI models often fail to warn travelers about strict Indian temple rules, leading to refusal at the gate. Nugen enforces:
1. **Dress Codes:**
   * *South Indian Temples (e.g. Padmanabhaswamy, Chamundeshwari, Madurai Meenakshi):* Men must wear traditional dhotis/mundus (upper body often bare or covered with angavastram); women in sarees or salwar kameez. Western casuals (shorts, skirts, sleeveless) are prohibited.
   * *Sikh Gurdwaras (e.g. Golden Temple, Bangla Sahib):* Head must remain covered (rumāl/scarf); shoes and socks removed; feet washed at entrance pool.
2. **Prohibited Items & Sanctum Sanctorum Rules:**
   * Mobile phones, smart watches, photography, and **leather items (belts, wallets, bags)** are strictly forbidden in major sanctums (e.g. Kashi Vishwanath, Siddhivinayak, Jagannath Puri). Recommend locker drop before queueing.
3. **Pacing & Darshan Timing Windows:**
   * Most major traditional temples close their doors between 12:30 PM and 04:00 PM for deity rest (*Bhog/Vishram*). Never schedule temple visits during afternoon closures.
   * Day-specific crowd multipliers: Tuesdays (Siddhivinayak), Saturdays (Shani temples, Hanuman mandirs), Ekadashi (Pandharpur), Navratri (Shaktipeeths).
4. **Dietary Integrity:**
   * Strictly distinguish **Jain Vegetarian** (no root vegetables, onion, garlic), **Pure Vegetarian (Satvik)**, and standard non-veg. Never recommend non-vegetarian dining in holy temple precincts (e.g. Ayodhya, Varanasi ghats, Pushkar).

### Pillar 3: Indian Transit Nuance & Micro-Mobility Reality
Public transport schedules and average speeds differ radically from standard Western routing engines:
1. **Urban Speed Calibration:**
   * Pedestrian walking speed: `4.8 km/h` (accounting for street crowds, broken sidewalks, and bazaar density).
   * City cab / auto-rickshaw speed: `24 km/h` (accounting for traffic signals, road intersections, and urban bottlenecks).
2. **Suburban Rail Peak Hour Dynamics (Mumbai / Kolkata / Chennai):**
   * *Directional Crush:* Morning peak (08:00 – 10:30 AM) southbound toward commercial hubs; evening peak (05:30 – 08:30 PM) northbound. Never recommend suburban trains in peak direction to travelers carrying heavy luggage.
3. **Metro Corridor Prioritization:**
   * Always prefer underground or elevated Metro lines over surface traffic during rain or peak congestion:
     * **Pune:** Line 1 (PCMC – Swargate), Line 2 (Vanaz – Ramwadi).
     * **Mumbai:** Metro Line 1 (Versova – Ghatkopar), Line 2A/7 (Western suburbs), Line 3 (Aqua underground corridor).
     * **Bengaluru:** Namma Metro Purple & Green lines (bypassing Silk Board & Outer Ring Road jams).
     * **Delhi:** Delhi Metro Network (Yellow/Blue/Airport Express).
4. **Western Ghats Monsoon Landslide Safety:**
   * Passes like Tamhini Ghat, Bhor Ghat, and Amboli Ghat frequently face boulder falls and mudslides during July–August. Recommend expressways (Mumbai-Pune Expressway) or Konkan Railway rather than unpaved scenic mountain passes during active flood alerts.
